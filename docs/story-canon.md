# Story canon

An internal reference for the recipe box's story, so later arcs stay consistent. **Contains spoilers.** The source of truth for wording is `src/story/` (chapters, scenes, notes); this file records what's *true* and what's deliberately *not decided*. Update it whenever an arc ships.

## Confirmed facts (through Arc 2)

- **The kitchen** is old and lived-in. Its recipe box's cards were wiped blank; baking a recipe brings its card back. Who wiped them, and how, is not explained.
- **Marmalade** is the kitchen's ginger chef-cat. She has lived here for years and knows every corner of the kitchen, not its history. She knew Edie (she waited by the oven for broken biscuits when "the leaves turned") without ever knowing her name. She remembers R. only as a grown man ("someone tall, on Sundays"). She has limits: she won't open the letter for M.
- **E. is Edie.** Edie wrote the margin notes, the scraps, the label inside the box lid, and the card "For M., when the box is full again." She signs in full only once, on her oat crisp card (Arc 2, Chapter 8). Her own mother taught her the oat crisps. Her hand is graphite pencil.
- **R. is Edie's son.** She packed his school lunches (the lunch bag, his first-week list, his crayon thank-you note to "Mum"). As a grown man he visited on Sundays and went home with a tin of biscuits. His favourites: the "window" biscuits (*Lunchbox Windows*) and, in Arc 1's note, the nutty ones with jam (the Peanut Butter & Jam secret). R. is not named beyond the initial.
- **The winter fair.** Edie ran a stall at the winter fair outside the kitchen window (the snowball scrap in Arc 1; the photograph in Arc 2). At the *first* fair R. sold out by noon and M. "ate the profits": the dark, sticky parkin squares.
- **The photograph** shows the kitchen, the fair table, Edie and young R. Its bottom-right corner, where M. would have been, is torn off.
- **The tin** was hidden behind the cupboard's deliberately loose back board ("Loose on purpose. Mind the tin."). It held, from the top: the blank oat crisp card, the lunch bag, R.'s card and first-week list, the photograph, and under the paper lining, the letter.
- **The letter for M.** is in Edie's hand, addressed to M. at a street since crossed out, stamped *Returned, not known at this address*, and **still sealed**. On the back, R. wrote: "Mum, keep it with the box. When M. comes back, it'll be for the box." So Edie and R. both knew about it, and M. was meant to inherit the box.
- **The brass key** (Arc 1) opened the old cupboard; its tag said "Not yet". Marmalade echoes that about the letter: "Not yet."

## Deliberately open

- **Who M. is**, and their relationship to Edie and R. Constraints: M. was at the first winter fair (young enough to eat the stall's profits); M. moved away from the address Edie had; M. was expected to come back; the box is meant for M. Nothing has said M.'s gender or name. Marmalade's Arc 1 aside ("I can think of one or two things that start with M.") is a joke about herself and should stay a joke, not a reveal.
- **What's in the letter.**
- **Who wiped the cards**, and why the box must be "full again".
- **What happened to Edie.** Never stated. Keep it gentle: the story is about what she left, not about loss.
- Whether the player has any connection to M. (Marmalade: "I don't think it's yours either. Not yet." keeps this possible, not promised).

## Timeline constraints

1. Edie's mother bakes oat crisps; Edie learns them.
2. The first winter fair: Edie, R. as a small boy, and M.
3. R.'s school years: packed lunches (Wednesdays were windows).
4. R. grown, visiting on Sundays; Marmalade is in the kitchen by then.
5. Edie writes to M.; the letter comes back. She hides the tin, writes "For M., when the box is full again", tapes the brass key behind it.
6. The kitchen stands quiet; the cards are blank; Marmalade stays.
7. The player arrives.

## Style rules for future arcs

- Edie's notes are short, practical, warm, a little dry; never explain themselves. R.'s are a child's crayon (simple, earnest) or a grown man's plain pencil.
- Marmalade's lines stay one or two sentences. She can remember small things and be moved; she never knows more than the kitchen could have told her.
- Notes never name an ingredient (a content test enforces it); clues describe a feeling or a moment.
- Each arc answers at least one question, complicates one, and leaves one strong hook. No new initials without a plan to pay them off.
